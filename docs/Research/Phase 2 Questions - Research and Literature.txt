Predictive Performance

Regime Detection Quality

Interpretability

Robustness

Early Detection

How expensive is it to train, run, and update?
Real-Time Suitability

Scalability

Implementation Complexity

Practical Value
Does the method actually improve the final decision-support system, rather than simply improving a technical metric?
Core evaluation lens

Performance + Financial Meaning + Uncertainty + Robustness + Interpretability + Practicality

6. Define Research-to-Decision Framework

Every method, technique, or idea we research will fall into one of four categories:

🟢 USE
Strong evidence and good fit for Project 1A.
We plan to include it in the solution.
Example: HMM shows strong suitability for regime detection.
🟡 TEST
Promising, but evidence is not strong enough to commit yet.
We will implement and experimentally evaluate it.
Example: Time-series foundation model looks promising but needs backtesting.
🔴 REJECT
Poor fit, weak evidence, excessive complexity, or doesn't solve our problem well.
We won't pursue it further.
Example: A method requires data we cannot reliably obtain.
🔵 KEEP OPEN
Interesting but not necessary to decide now.
Keep it as a future enhancement or alternative.
Example: A newer research technique that could be explored later.
Decision flow

Research Evidence
↓
Evaluate against criteria
↓
Assess Project 1A fit
↓
USE / TEST / REJECT / KEEP OPEN

Research does not automatically become a feature. Evidence must justify the decision.

7. Define Evidence Standard

Methodological Evidence
Is the method theoretically suitable for our problem?
Empirical Evidence
Has the method demonstrated useful results in experiments or real financial datasets?
Financial Evidence
Does it make sense in actual market conditions, not just on generic datasets?
Comparative Evidence
Does it perform better or provide a meaningful advantage compared with simpler alternatives?
Robustness Evidence
Does the result hold across different time periods, market conditions, and stress periods?
Reproducibility Evidence
Can we reproduce the reported methodology and reasonably verify its results?
Data Compatibility
Can we obtain the required data with sufficient quality and consistency?
Operational Evidence
Can the method realistically operate within our intended system architecture?
Risk Evidence
Have we considered overfitting, leakage, instability, uncertainty, and other failure modes?
Business / Decision Evidence
Does the method ultimately improve the usefulness of the decision-support system?
Evidence strength

Strong Evidence
→ Multiple credible sources + empirical support + financial relevance + robustness
→ Can influence architecture/model decisions.

Moderate Evidence
→ Credible research + promising results, but requires our own testing
→ Usually becomes TEST.

Weak Evidence
→ Theoretical claim, isolated result, blog/tutorial, or unsupported opinion
→ Useful for exploration, not enough for major decisions.
